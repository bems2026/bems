How a reading becomes a chart

Four stages, one Raspberry Pi. The live path never leaves the building.

1  Field Your devices declared in one directory · 2.4 GHz, same L2 segment as the Pi · no cloud in the read path
metering outlets switched circuits CT branch meters air-conditioning (IR) sensors your circuit tree → derived phase totals
Tuya v3.4/v3.5 · TCP 6668 · discovery UDP 6666/6667 every ~5 s
2  One Raspberry Pi Four processes, each with one job systemd · restarts on boot · nothing else is allowed to reach the bridge
Node-RED bridge:1880

Talks to the devices. Shapes one payload for everything downstream. Bound to loopback.

Auth proxy:8080

Authenticates every request and every socket. Validates and audits commands.

Ingest daemon60 s

Writes history. Buffers to disk through a database outage and drains oldest first.

Scheduler15 s

Fires schedules and auto-shed through the same gate a human click uses.

WS /ws/live every 2 s upsert readings + totals
3  LiveBrowser & kiosk display

Seven screens, a 3D floor view, and a control page that says which classes are really live. Falls back to 15 s polling when the socket drops.

4  HistorySupabase Postgres

30 days per-minute, then rolled into permanent hourly buckets. Charts read it straight from the browser — not through the proxy.

Cut the internet and the building keeps working: metering, control and shedding are all local. Only history and login need the outside.
